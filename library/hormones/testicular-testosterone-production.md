# Testicular testosterone production

**One-liner:** Leydig cells in the testis make about 95% of a man's testosterone, on an LH command, through a single mitochondrial gate.

**The command chain**
1. The **hypothalamus** pulses **GnRH**.
2. The **pituitary** releases **LH**. LH arrives in pulses, which is why blood T wobbles minute to minute.
3. LH binds **LHCGR** on Leydig cells, and **cAMP** rises inside them.
4. Within minutes, the cell makes more **StAR**, so more cholesterol reaches **CYP11A1** and becomes pregnenolone. **This is the rate-limiting step.**
5. Over hours, the cell makes more of the enzymes: CYP17A1 → DHEA → 3β-HSD2 → androstenedione → **17β-HSD3** (testis-specific) → testosterone.
6. **Feedback:** testosterone, and the estradiol made from it, slow GnRH and LH. That's a steady negative loop with a morning peak.

**Key numbers**
- **Output:** ~5–7 mg/day (3–10 mg/day by tracer studies). About 4% becomes DHT and ~0.2% becomes estradiol.
- **Inside the testis:** T is about **40–100× higher than in blood**. Sperm production (via Sertoli cells) needs that local flood.
- **Why outside testosterone lowers sperm counts:** extra T switches off LH, the Leydig cells quiet down, and the testis gradient collapses (Coviello et al. 2004).

**Plain-language rate-limiting summary:** the factory has lots of machinery, but only one door. LH controls how often the door opens.
