# Isoflavones & phytoestrogens — stub card

**One-liner:** soy isoflavones (genistein, daidzein and the gut metabolite equol) are plant molecules that bind estrogen receptors weakly. They are a different class from pharmaceutical or cosmetic estradiol, and the dose gap between the two is large.

**Status:** stub, 2026-11-02. Full treatment: [`white-papers/soy-estrogen-male-exogenous.md`](../../white-papers/soy-estrogen-male-exogenous.md) §1–3, §8 · sources: [`sources/soy-estrogen-male.md`](../../sources/soy-estrogen-male.md)

**Molecules**
- Genistein and daidzein come from soy foods.
- Equol is made from daidzein by gut bacteria in some people: about 25–30% of Western adults vs 50–60% in Japan, Korea and China (Setchell & Clerici 2010, PMID 20519412). **HUMAN**

**Intake**
- Poland: measured ~0.16 mg/day (Witkowska et al. 2015, PMID 26569297). National representative data beyond that study: UNSOURCED (paper §3).
- Japanese men: median 18 mg/day (Food Safety Commission of Japan, 2006).

**Male hormones**
- In meta-analyses, soy and isoflavones did not change testosterone, free T, SHBG or estrogens in men (Reed et al. 2021, PMID 33383165). **HUMAN**
