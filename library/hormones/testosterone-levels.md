# Testosterone levels: ranges, binding, and rhythm (both sexes)

**One-liner:** Men run about 10–15× more testosterone than women. Most of it rides on carrier proteins, and in young men it peaks in the morning.

**Key numbers**

| Measure | Men | Women (premenopausal) |
|---|---|---|
| Total T, typical range | **264–916 ng/dL** (≈ 9.2–31.8 nmol/L), median ~530. Harmonized, non-obese, age 19–39 | **~20–70 ng/dL** (0.6–2.5 nmol/L) |
| LC-MS 95% range | 7.7–29.4 nmol/L (age 18–40) | 0–1.7 nmol/L (under 40) |
| Daily production | ~5–7 mg (estimates 3–10) | ~0.1–0.4 mg |
| Where it comes from | >95% testis | ovary + adrenal + conversion of precursors: ~25/25/50 (Burger 2002) or roughly equal thirds (Handelsman 2018). Estimates differ by method |

**Free vs bound (blood carriage)**
- **SHBG** (sex hormone-binding globulin, made in the liver) binds tightly: about **44%**.
- **Albumin** binds loosely: about **50%** (33–54% across studies). It can let go in tissue capillaries, so it counts as part of "bioavailable" T.
- **Free:** about **1–3%**.
- SHBG rises with estrogen and thyroid hormone. It falls with obesity, insulin resistance, and inflammation. The same total T can therefore mean different free T.

**Daily rhythm**
- In men aged 30–40, T is **20–25% lower at 4 p.m. than at 8 a.m.** By age 70 the gap is only about 10%. That's why tests are drawn in the morning.
- Women also show an early-morning peak, plus a small mid-cycle rise.

**Watch-out:** labs differ. Immunoassays read low female levels poorly, so mass spectrometry (LC-MS) is preferred. Always check the lab's own range and units (ng/dL × 0.0347 = nmol/L).

**Sources:** Travison et al. 2017 (10.1210/jc.2016-2935); Handelsman et al. 2018 (10.1210/er.2018-00020); Burger 2002 (10.1016/S0015-0282(02)02985-0); Dunn et al. 1981 (10.1210/jcem-53-1-58); Goldman et al. 2017 (10.1210/er.2017-00025); Brambilla et al. 2009 (10.1210/jc.2008-1902).
