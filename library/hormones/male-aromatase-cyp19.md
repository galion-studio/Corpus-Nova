# Male aromatase (CYP19A1): the quiet estrogen factory in a man

---
**Corpus Nova** · Theme 1 (BioChem) · Chemistry card · Nova Sheet · Universal readers · Voice: easy, abstract, a little fun  
**License:** MIT · 2026-11-02
---

**One-liner:** Men make estradiol all the time. One enzyme, **aromatase (CYP19A1)**, snips one carbon off testosterone and makes ring A flat and aromatic. Most of that happens **outside the testis**, in muscle, fat, bone and brain, from testosterone that left the testis first.

**The chemistry (three bites, one enzyme)**
- testosterone → **estradiol (E2)** · androstenedione → **estrone (E1)** (Ghosh 2009).
- Three oxidations, each using **1 O₂ + 1 NADPH**. Two of them chew on the C19 methyl group, and the third makes ring A aromatic (Ghosh 2009). C₁₉ → C₁₈: one carbon gone.
- 17β-HSD can turn E1 into E2 (Sasano 1997; de Ronde 2011).

| Testis vein vs arm vein E2 | **~50×** higher | HUMAN ♂ | Kelch 1972 |

\* derived: our arithmetic on the source's numbers.

**Where the factory runs:** Leydig cells in the testis (Brodie 2001) · fat and muscle (Longcope 1978) · bone cells (Sasano 1997; Schweikert 1995) · brain cortex and hippocampus, the same in men and women (Stoffel-Wagner 1999). Each tissue flips its own switch, a tissue-specific exon 1/promoter on the same gene (Harada 1993).

**Two different "small numbers."** "Only a sliver of T becomes E2" (~0.2–0.4%) and "most E2 is made outside the testis" (~73–80%) are both true. One is a slice of a big river, the other is most of a small stream.

**Watch-out:** the popular "85% peripheral" figure is **UNSOURCED** here. Local tissue E2 can be high without changing blood levels (Simpson 2002). Research summary, not medical advice.
