# GnRH receptor (GNRHR) — plus its upstream partner KISS1R

**Plain idea:** The brain runs reproduction with a drumbeat. The hypothalamus releases gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) in pulses. GNRHR on pituitary cells hears each beat and answers with LH and FSH. A steady beat works. A constant flood shuts it off.

**Exact name:** Gonadotropin-releasing hormone receptor · gene `GNRHR` · class A GPCR · Gq/11 → PLC → IP₃ → Ca²⁺ (the same family of signal as OXTR) · ligand: GnRH (GnRH-I).

**Odd feature:** The human type I GnRH receptor lacks the usual C-terminal tail that most GPCRs use to switch themselves off quickly. This affects how it desensitizes. Clinically, long-acting GnRH drugs overstimulate it until the pituitary goes quiet. That is how "chemical castration" for prostate cancer and puberty blockers work.

**Upstream:** Kisspeptin → **KISS1R** (old name GPR54) on GnRH neurons. People with broken KISS1R don't start puberty on their own. The pituitary itself still works when GnRH is supplied (shown in mice lacking the receptor).

**Where it shows up:** Anterior pituitary gonadotropes (main). KISS1R: hypothalamic GnRH neurons.
