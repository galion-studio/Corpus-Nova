# G-protein-coupled estrogen receptor 1 (GPER1, formerly GPR30)

**Plain idea:** Most estrogen locks live in the nucleus and work slowly. GPER1 is the fast one. It is a membrane-type receptor (a GPCR), which sends quick signals in seconds.

**Exact name:** GPER1 · gene `GPER1` (old name GPR30) · class A GPCR · ligand: 17β-estradiol; selective research agonist G-1.

**How it works:** Estrogen binds → G-proteins fire → Ca²⁺ rises and PI3K makes PIP₃ (a fast signalling lipid). The famous 2005 surprise: much of GPER1 sits *inside* the cell, on the endoplasmic reticulum (the cell's protein factory membrane), not only on the surface.

**Where it shows up:** Reproductive, nervous, endocrine, immune and cardiovascular systems, in both sexes.

**Honest caveat:** GPER1 is real and well-studied, but which estrogen effects need GPER1 rather than membrane ERα is still argued over, tissue by tissue. Treat map tags as "present and active", not "the main driver".

**Body-map tags:** uterus.endometrium (fast estrogen signals)

**Key sources:** Revankar et al. 2005 · Prossnitz & Barton 2011
