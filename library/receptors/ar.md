# Androgen receptor (AR)

**Plain idea:** AR is the lock that testosterone and its stronger cousin DHT fit into. Once loaded, it walks into the cell's nucleus (its gene library) and switches genes on or off. Everyone has it — males and females.

**Exact name:** Androgen receptor · gene `AR` · nuclear receptor **NR3C4** · gene sits on the X chromosome · ligands: testosterone, 5α-dihydrotestosterone (DHT binds about 2× tighter and lets go about 5× slower than testosterone).

**How it works — two speeds:**
- **Slow (genomic, hours):** AR waits in the cytoplasm held by chaperone proteins (helper proteins that keep it folded) → androgen binds → AR pairs up → enters the nucleus → binds androgen response elements (short DNA "address labels") → gene reading changes.
- **Fast (non-genomic, seconds–minutes):** AR near the membrane switches on kinase chains (Src → ERK) and Ca²⁺ entry without touching DNA. Shown clearly in Sertoli cells.

**Where it shows up:**
- Male: Sertoli cells, Leydig cells, epididymis, prostate, seminal vesicles, penile erectile tissue, muscle, bone, skin, brain (hypothalamus, preoptic area)
- Female: ovarian theca and early follicles, uterus, breast, muscle, bone, brain
- Distribution is shared; **levels of ligand** (adult men have many times more circulating testosterone than women) and local enzymes (5α-reductase makes DHT; aromatase turns testosterone into estradiol) make the big difference.

**Receptor beats hormone:** In complete androgen insensitivity syndrome, a 46,XY person makes plenty of testosterone but has a non-working AR, and develops a female body form. The lock matters more than the key.

**Why connection cares:** Androgens shape the brain's mating and rivalry circuits and feed back on GnRH neurons; AR is one of the receptor dials behind sex differences in social behaviour. Much of testosterone's brain action in rodents runs through *estrogen* receptors after aromatase — see `esr1.md`.
