# Estrogen receptor α (ERα)

**Plain idea:** ERα is the main lock for estradiol, the strongest estrogen. It grows tissues — the uterine lining, breast ducts, bone — and it matters in males too.

**Exact name:** Estrogen receptor alpha · gene `ESR1` · nuclear receptor NR3A1 · ligand: 17β-estradiol (also many drugs and plant/industrial "estrogen mimics").

**How it works:** Four routes, per Hewitt & Korach 2018:
- **Genomic:** binds estrogen response elements (EREs) on DNA, helped by "pioneer" proteins (e.g. FOXA1) that open packed DNA.
- **Tethered:** holds onto another DNA-binding protein instead of DNA directly.
- **Non-genomic:** a membrane-docked pool fires fast kinase signals.
- **Ligand-independent:** growth-factor kinases can switch it on with no estrogen at all.

**Where it shows up:**
- Female: uterus (endometrium + myometrium), cervix, vagina, fallopian tube, breast ducts, ovary theca, hypothalamus, pituitary
- Male: efferent ducts and epididymis, prostate stroma, bone growth plates, hypothalamus
- Both: bone, liver, fat, blood vessels, brain

**Male surprise:** Male mice without ERα can't soak up fluid in the efferent ducts. Sperm arrive diluted and fertility falls (Hess et al. 1997). A man with no working ERα grew tall with open growth plates and thin bones (Smith et al. 1994): estrogen closes male growth plates.

**Why connection cares:** Estradiol through ERα raises OXTR numbers in parts of the rat brain. Mothers who give more care carry more of this estrogen-boosted OXTR (Champagne et al. 2001). That links the steroid layer to the bonding layer.
