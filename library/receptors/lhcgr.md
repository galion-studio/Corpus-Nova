# LH/choriogonadotropin receptor (LHCGR)

**Plain idea:** LHCGR is the doorbell that luteinizing hormone (LH) rings. In testes it says "make testosterone". In ovaries it says "make androgens", then "ovulate", then "make progesterone". In early pregnancy, the embryo's hCG rings the same bell.

**Exact name:** Luteinizing hormone/choriogonadotropin receptor · gene `LHCGR` · class A GPCR with a large hormone-catching outer domain (leucine-rich repeats) · ligands: LH and hCG · main signal: Gs → adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA.

**Where it shows up:**
- Male: Leydig cells (testis)
- Female: theca cells; granulosa cells of large, pre-ovulatory follicles; corpus luteum

**Why it matters:** In Leydig cells, cAMP from LHCGR speeds up cholesterol's trip into mitochondria. That is the slow step of testosterone making. Gene changes that switch LHCGR on with no LH cause early puberty in boys; changes that break it block male development.
