# Testosterone production — receptor by receptor

**Plain idea:** Testosterone is made on request. A chain of four receptors passes the request from brain to testis, then a fifth (AR) hears the answer.

**The chain (male):**
1. **KISS1R** on GnRH neurons — kisspeptin starts the pulses (no KISS1R → no puberty).
2. **GNRHR** on pituitary gonadotropes — GnRH pulses → LH + FSH.
3. **LHCGR** on Leydig cells — LH → Gs → cAMP → PKA.
4. Inside the Leydig cell:
   - cAMP speeds cholesterol into the mitochondria (cell power units). StAR and a TSPO-containing protein complex do the moving. This is the slow, rate-setting step.
   - CYP11A1 cuts cholesterol into pregnenolone.
   - Enzymes in the smooth endoplasmic reticulum finish the job: pregnenolone → testosterone.
5. **FSHR + AR** on Sertoli cells — FSH and the very high testosterone inside the testis support sperm making. Remove AR from Sertoli cells only (mouse "SCARKO"), and sperm making stops in meiosis.

**Then:** In the blood, testosterone reaches AR in target tissues. Locally it can be upgraded to DHT (5α-reductase → stronger AR signal) or converted to estradiol (aromatase → ER signal). Testosterone and estradiol feed back on the hypothalamus and pituitary to slow the chain.

**Two Leydig generations:** Fetal Leydig cells make the androgen surge that builds male genitals and masculinizes the brain. Adult Leydig cells grow from stem cells after birth and drive puberty. Once formed, they rarely turn over. Output declines in many ageing men.

**Female note:** Ovaries make androgens too. Theca cells use the same LHCGR → cAMP route, and granulosa cells then turn the androgens into estradiol (`fshr.md`).
