# Laterality (left vs right bone volume)

- **Perfect symmetry is rare.** The asymmetry score is %DA = (R − L)/mean × 100.
- **Arms lean right.** In 780 adults the median humeral midshaft breadth was **+2.98%** to the right and the radius **+2.21%**. 76% of people were right-biased at the humerus, and over 10% asymmetry was common in the arms [Auerbach & Ruff 2006].
- **Legs nearly match.** Femoral and tibial shafts were **−0.49% / −0.78%** (a slight left tilt). Median leg-length asymmetry never passed 0.71% (about 2.5 mm) [Auerbach & Ruff 2006].
- **Sport shows the effect strongly.** In former tennis players, the playing arm had cortical area **+12–32%** and total area **+16–21%**, with volumetric density about the same. The extra is size, not density [Haapasalo 2000]. Former baseball pitchers kept **55.5%** of the size benefit after 50+ years [Warden 2014]. Girls who trained before puberty gained mostly by periosteal (outward) growth [Bass 2002].
- **Hips:** left and right correlate closely (r = 0.92–0.95), yet more than half of people differ by more than scanner noise. 7% of patients with osteopenic spines had osteoporosis in one hip [Mounach 2012].
- **Handedness:** left-handers can show reversed asymmetry in the hand bones (metacarpals) [Roy 1994, via Auerbach & Ruff 2006].
- **Sex:** median humeral asymmetry was +3.35% in men vs +2.29% in women [Auerbach & Ruff 2006].
- **Reading tip:** a mild left-right difference can simply be loading history. It is not automatically disease. Compare sectors *and* sides before treating one value as "the" bone.
