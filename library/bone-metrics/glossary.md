# Bone volume glossary

Short, universal definitions. The deeper comparisons are in the Theme 4 white paper (`white-papers/bone-volume-sectors-laterality.md`). Symbols follow the standard nomenclature [Dempster 2013; Bouxsein 2010].

- **TV (total volume):** the whole region you drew around the bone tissue of interest.
- **BV (bone volume):** how much of that region is actual mineralized bone.
- **BV/TV:** the fraction of the region that is bone. It measures how full the box is, not body weight. Example: young men's wrist trabecular BV/TV is about 26% higher than young women's [Khosla 2006].
- **Ct.V (cortical volume):** volume of the compact outer shell. About 80% of the adult skeleton is cortical [Clarke 2008].
- **Tb.V (trabecular volume):** volume of the inner spongy lattice, about 20% of the skeleton [Clarke 2008].
- **Tb.N / Tb.Th / Tb.Sp:** trabecular number (how many struts), thickness (how thick each strut is), and separation (the gaps between struts).
- **CSA / Tt.Ar / Ct.Ar (cross-sectional areas):** the area of one slice, either total or cortical only. Stack slices and you get volume.
- **Ct.Th (cortical thickness):** how thick the shell is. It can thin with age even while the outer size grows [Riggs 2004].
- **Periosteal / endosteal envelope:** the outer surface and the inner (marrow-facing) surface of the cortex, where bone is added or removed.
- **aBMD vs vBMD:** areal density (g/cm², from DXA) vs volumetric density (mg/cm³, from QCT). Areal density is biased by bone size [Carter 1992].
- **BMAD:** bone mineral apparent density, a size-adjusted estimate made from DXA [Carter 1992].
- **%DA (directional asymmetry):** (Right − Left) / mean × 100. Positive means the right side is bigger [Auerbach & Ruff 2006].
