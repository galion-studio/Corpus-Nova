# Measuring bone volume

- **DXA (dual X-ray):** good for maps of areal mineral "density." It flattens 3D into 2D, so it does not measure true volume, and bigger bones look denser [Carter 1992].
- **QCT / pQCT:** X-ray CT calibrated for bone. It reports true volumetric density and areas and separates cortical from trabecular bone [Riggs 2004; Haapasalo 2000].
- **HR-pQCT:** high-resolution CT of the wrist or ankle (89 µm voxels in Khosla 2006). It shows trabecular number and thickness and cortical porosity [Khosla 2006; Zebaze 2010].
- **μCT:** very fine structure, usually in lab samples or animals. The nomenclature guide is rodent-based [Bouxsein 2010].
- **MRI:** soft-tissue friendly. It shows bone geometry and marrow but not mineral [Bass 2002 used MRI for geometry].

**Why the number changes with the tool:**
- **Tennis arm.** It "looks" denser on DXA, but its vBMD is nearly equal on pQCT [Haapasalo 2000].
- **Porosity.** Cortical density loss is **15%** if pores are ignored and **43%** if they are counted [Zebaze 2010].

Always ask: which volume, which sector, which side, which sex, which tool?
