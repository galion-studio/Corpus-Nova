# Sectors of a long bone

A bone is not one number. Volume changes along its length and between tissue types. Figure: `figures/bone-volume/bone-volume-sector-map.png`.

1. **Proximal / midshaft / distal:** three sectors along the length. The ends, near the joints, hold more trabecular volume. The midshaft is cortical-heavy.
2. **Epiphysis / metaphysis / diaphysis:** the anatomy names for the end, the flare and the shaft. The diaphysis is mostly cortical. The metaphysis and epiphysis are trabecular inside a thin shell [Clarke 2008].
3. **Cortical vs trabecular compartments:** an outer shell and an inner lattice, with different jobs and different volumes. Cortical:trabecular ratios are **95:5** in the radial shaft, **50:50** in the femoral head and **25:75** in a vertebra [Clarke 2008]. The vertebral shell is only about **0.38 mm** thick [Eswaran 2006].
4. **Periosteal vs endosteal envelopes:** the outer and inner surfaces. Over adult life bone area grows about **15–16%** while the cortex drifts outward [Riggs 2004]. Inner-wall remodelling caused **47%** of distal-radius bone loss in women [Zebaze 2010].

**Not available:** a published human table of Ct.V and Tb.V for each third of whole long bones. We found none, so the site ratios above stand in for it.
