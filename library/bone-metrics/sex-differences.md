# Sex differences in bone metrics

Scope: bone numbers only. Hormones and reproductive anatomy are covered by other themes' library entries.

## Peak bone mass
- Total-body bone mineral plateaus about 6 years after the growth-spurt peak: **around age 18 in girls and 20 in boys**. Bone *area* plateaus 1–2 years before bone *mineral* [Baxter-Jones 2011].
- In the 4 years around that growth-spurt peak, both sexes lay down about **39%** of adult total-body mineral. The figure is **33%** at the femoral neck, and **43% (male) / 46% (female)** at the spine and total hip [Baxter-Jones 2011].

## Size and geometry
- Young men's bone cross-sectional areas are **35–42% larger** than young women's [Riggs 2004, QCT].
- Both sexes widen with age by about **15–16%** [Riggs 2004].
- Because bigger bones look denser on DXA, compare the sexes with volumetric or size-adjusted measures [Carter 1992].

## Cortical vs trabecular
- **Trabecular vBMD loss over life:** **−55% (women) vs −46% (men)** at central sites, and about −24% vs −26% at peripheral sites [Riggs 2004].
- **Cortical vBMD loss:** about **−25% (women) vs −18% (men)** [Riggs 2004].
- **Wrist microstructure** [Khosla 2006, HR-pQCT, 602 adults]:
  - Young men start with **+26% BV/TV** and **+28% thicker struts**, with a similar number of struts.
  - Over life, women lose struts (number −13%, spacing +24%). Men mainly thin them (−24% vs −18%).
  - BV/TV falls about equally (−27% / −26%), but losing struts hurts strength more.
- **Femoral neck:** thinning of the superoposterior cortex with age is larger in women [Mayhew 2005].

## Laterality
- Humeral asymmetry: **+3.35% in men vs +2.29% in women** [Auerbach & Ruff 2006].
