# Redox pairs (NAD⁺/NADH & glutathione)

**One-liner:** Cells move electrons the way a city moves packages: with named carriers, separate depots, and careful hand-offs.

**In plain words**
- **NAD⁺** picks up electrons during fuel breakdown and becomes **NADH**. It then hands them to the mitochondrial chain, which ends at oxygen.
- **Glutathione (GSH)** is the cell's redox buffer. It keeps proteins and membranes from being oxidized by accident.

**Key numbers**
- Free NAD⁺/NADH is about **700 in the cytosol** but only **~7–8 in mitochondria**. That's from classic rat-liver work (Williamson et al. 1967). Same molecule, different room, ~100× different "electron pressure."
- GSH is about **1–10 mM** in cells and kept mostly reduced, often **>100 GSH : 1 GSSG** in the cytosol (Schafer & Buettner 2001).

**ROS (reactive oxygen species):** electrons that leak off early make superoxide and H₂O₂. At low, local levels, H₂O₂ is a normal **signal** (Sies & Jones 2020). It becomes damage only when production outruns cleanup (Murphy 2009).

**Watch-out:** "antioxidant" headlines oversimplify. The useful story is **controlled** redox, not zero oxidation.
