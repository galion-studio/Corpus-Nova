# Blood buffers (bicarbonate)

**One-liner:** Your blood keeps a near-steady pH by trading CO₂ and bicarbonate like a chemical shock absorber.

**In plain words:** Acids appear all day from metabolism. Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) soaks them up; the lungs then blow off extra CO₂ so the buffer can reset.

**Key numbers (order of magnitude):** Arterial blood pH ~7.35–7.45. The main open buffer is CO₂ ↔ H₂CO₃ ↔ HCO₃⁻, watched by lungs and kidneys.

**Why it matters:** Small pH drifts change how proteins fold and how oxygen rides on hemoglobin.
