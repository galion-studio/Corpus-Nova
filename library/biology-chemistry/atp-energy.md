# ATP: the body's spendable energy coin

**One-liner:** Cells don't store power in a big battery. They keep recharging a tiny pool of ATP and spend it on almost every job.

**In plain words**
- Burning food recharges **ADP → ATP**, and every job pays by splitting ATP back.
- The pool is small but turns over fast. A resting adult recycles roughly **their own body weight in ATP each day** (Törnroth-Horsefield & Neutze 2008).

**Where the energy goes (resting body)**
- **Na⁺/K⁺ pumps:** ~19–28% of resting energy use.
- **Protein synthesis:** ~25–30% (Rolfe & Brown 1997).

**Three ways to make ATP**

| Route | Speed | Yield | Needs O₂? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creatine phosphate (PCr, ~20–30 mM in muscle) | instant (seconds) | 1 ATP per PCr | no |
| Glycolysis | fast | 2 ATP per glucose | no |
| Oxidative phosphorylation | slower, huge | ~30–32 ATP per glucose in total (Hinkle 2005) | yes |

**Watch-out:** lactate isn't just waste. It's a fuel shuttled through the blood to heart and muscle (Brooks 2018; Hui et al. 2017).
