# Cytoplasm: jelly, honey, or sponge?

**What it is:** Cytoplasm is *viscoelastic* — part flowing liquid (*viscous*, like honey), part springy solid (*elastic*, like jelly). Which one you feel depends on your size and how fast you move.

**Size matters:** In human HeLa cells, probes smaller than the mesh (~5 nm) feel ~2 mPa·s — a few times water. Large probes feel ~44 mPa·s, about 20× more (Kalwarczyk et al. 2011).

**Time matters:** Poke a cell fast and it resists like a water-soaked sponge; wait and water seeps through the pores and it relaxes. That's *poroelastic* behavior, seen at times below ~0.5 s (Moeendarbary et al. 2013).

**Activity matters:** Bacterial cytoplasm acts like a glass-forming liquid — things above ~30 nm get caged, and metabolism "fluidizes" it (Parry et al. 2014). In yeast, the growth regulator mTORC1 sets ribosome crowding; inhibiting it about doubled how fast ≥20 nm particles moved (Delarue et al. 2018).
