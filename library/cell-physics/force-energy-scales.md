# Force and energy scales in the cell

**What it is:** Everything in a cell is priced in **k_BT** — the energy of heat jiggle. At body temperature (310 K), k_BT ≈ 4.3 pN·nm (our arithmetic). A piconewton (pN) is a trillionth of a newton; a nanometer (nm) a billionth of a meter.

**Machines on the same scale (sourced):**
- Kinesin (cargo-carrying motor): stalls at ~5.5–7 pN, 8 nm steps (Visscher et al. 1999). One step ≈ 11 k_BT.
- Myosin (muscle motor), single molecule: ~3–4 pN, ~11 nm steps (Finer et al. 1994).

**Why it matters:** Motors work only ~10× above thermal noise — they operate inside the storm, not above it. And at cell size, friction beats momentum (*low Reynolds number*, Purcell 1977): stop pushing and you stop at once. These units link heart-muscle force, axon transport, and membrane mechanics on the body map.
