# Condensates, crowding, and the aggregation risk

**What it is:** *Biomolecular condensates* are droplets inside cells with no membrane around them — like oil drops in vinegar. They gather specific proteins and RNA (Banani et al. 2017). Crowding pushes them to form.

**Crowding as a switch (human-relevant):** When mammalian cells shrink in extra-salty surroundings, the protein DCP1A forms droplets within ~10 s and dissolves ~100 s after conditions return to normal. How much forms tracks how much the cell shrank. In the nucleus, a similar effect on CPSF6 disrupted how gene readouts end (Jalihal et al. 2020). Ribosome crowding also tunes condensates in yeast and human cells (Delarue et al. 2018).

**Disease hook (high level):** The ALS-linked protein FUS forms liquid droplets that, in the test tube, age into solid fibers; patient mutations speed this up (Patel et al. 2015). ALS kills the nerve cells that control muscles. Crowding is not the single cause of such diseases, but it is part of the physical stage they play on.
