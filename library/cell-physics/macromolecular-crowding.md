# Macromolecular crowding

**What it is:** Inside a cell, proteins, RNA, and ribosomes take up a big share of the space. Water is still there, but free room is scarce — like a sold-out concert hall with few open seats. Physicists call the blocked space *excluded volume*: two molecules can't sit in the same spot.

**Why it matters:** Crowding changes how molecules move, meet, fold, and stick. It favors compact shapes and bound pairs, and makes each molecule act "more concentrated" than it is. Lab tests in dilute buffer can miss all of this.

**Key scales (sourced):**
- ~300–400 g/L of macromolecules in *E. coli* cytoplasm (Zimmerman & Trach 1991).
- ~20–30% of cell volume occupied by macromolecules (Ellis 2001).
- Typical lab sample: well under 1% of volume.

**It changes with cell volume:** Swelling a cell dilutes the crowd; shrinking packs it. GFP moved 4.7× faster as CHO cells went from half to twice normal volume (Swaminathan et al. 1997).
