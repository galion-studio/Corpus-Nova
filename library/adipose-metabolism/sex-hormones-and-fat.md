# Sex hormones and where fat is built

**Map job:** drive male / female (or dual-layer) fat-depot views.  
**Coordinates with:** `library/hormones/` (androgen & estrogen pathways — do not duplicate full hormone encyclopedias here).

## The short story
Men and women do not just carry different *amounts* of fat. They often build it in different **neighborhoods**.

- **Gynoid pattern** (more common in premenopausal women): surplus energy prefers **hips, buttocks, and thighs** (gluteofemoral subcutaneous fat).
- **Android pattern** (more common in men, and in many women after menopause): more **central** storage — abdominal subcutaneous belt plus a larger **visceral** share around organs.

Same TAG chemistry. Different addresses. Address changes risk and signaling.

## Estrogen (estradiol and friends)
- Favors **lower-body subcutaneous** storage and helps keep the **visceral** share relatively lower during reproductive years.
- When ovarian estrogen falls (**menopause**), many women shift toward a more central / visceral pattern — one reason metabolic risk can climb even if the bathroom scale barely moves.
- Estrogen signaling also matters in males; blocking it can increase fat mass. Details of receptors and levels → hormones library.

## Androgens (testosterone and DHT)
- In **men**, healthy-range testosterone is linked with less visceral pile-up; low testosterone often tracks with more central fat. Androgens can damp **LPL** activity and **adipogenesis** in cultured fat-cell precursors (depot- and sex-dependent).
- In **women**, *elevated* androgens (as in many cases of PCOS) often track with a more abdominal / visceral pattern — a reminder that “more androgen” is not one story across sexes.

## Local steroid workshop
Fat tissue is not only a *target* of sex hormones. It can make and convert them locally (aromatase: androgen → estrogen). So the map should treat depots as places with their own hormone microclimate, not just mirrors of a blood test.

## Brown / beige note
Heat-making fat near neck and collarbones appears in both sexes on cold-activated scans. Sex differences exist in studies; for the map, expose the **brown layer** as a shared cold-defense overlay with optional sex annotations later.
