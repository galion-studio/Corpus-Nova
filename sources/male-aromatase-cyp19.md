We checked every DOI, PMID and quoted number on 2026-11-02 against the Europe PMC and PubMed records and abstracts. For [4] and [17] we also read the open full text in PMC. If an entry says "no abstract", we quote no numbers from it. Numbers marked *derived* are our own ratios or unit conversions of a source's numbers. **Every entry has a species tag.** This file does not cover dosing, testing advice or treatment. Aromatase-inhibitor papers appear only for their background physiology.

**Unit key.** Molar masses (PubChem): testosterone C₁₉H₂₈O₂ 288.4 g/mol · androstenedione C₁₉H₂₆O₂ 286.4 g/mol · estradiol C₁₈H₂₄O₂ 272.4 g/mol · estrone C₁₈H₂₂O₂ 270.4 g/mol. E2: 1 pg/mL ≈ 3.67 pmol/L. 1 µg E2 ≈ 3.67 nmol.

**Two metrics that are easy to confuse.**
- **(a) Conversion share of T output.** What fraction of the testosterone a man makes ends up as E2? Paper 01 §7 gives ~0.2% (Handelsman 2026). Tracer studies give the transfer constant [ρ]BB T→E2 = 0.39% [1].
- **(b) Origin share of circulating E2.** What fraction of the E2 in blood came from peripheral aromatization, and what fraction was secreted by the testis [2, 4, 5]?

They answer different questions, so don't swap one for the other.

## A. Production rates and the origin split (HUMAN, men)
1. Longcope C, Kato T, Horton R. Conversion of blood androgens to estrogens in normal adult men and women. *J Clin Invest* 1969;48:2191–2201. doi:10.1172/jci106185. PMID 5355335 · PMC297476 (scanned PDF only, so we read the abstract). **HUMAN**, constant infusion of ³H-androstenedione and ³H-testosterone. Peripheral androgens are converted to estrogens and "contribute to the circulating blood levels." Conversion ratio, men vs women: **androstenedione→E1 0.013 vs 0.007** and **T→E2 0.0018 vs 0.005**. Transfer constant [ρ]BB, men vs women: **A→E1 1.35% vs 0.74%** and **T→E2 0.39% vs 0.15%**. Androstenedione is aromatized "to a greater degree" than T. Androgen interconversion in men: [ρ]BB A→T 4.5%, T→A 8.2%.
2. MacDonald PC, Madden JD, Brenner PF, Wilson JD, Siiteri PK. Origin of estrogen in normal men and in women with testicular feminization. *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 1979;49:905–916. doi:10.1210/jcem-49-6-905. PMID 511978. **HUMAN** (4 young adult men). Mean production rates: **E1 58 µg/24 h** and **E2 44 µg/24 h**. All E1 was accounted for by extraglandular formation. **"Only 12 µg or less"** of E2 production could not be explained by extraglandular formation from plasma C19 precursors, and the authors presume it came from testicular secretion. Plasma T production rate **5.7 mg/24 h**. *Derived: testis-secreted E2 ≤ 12/44 ≈ **≤27%**, so extraglandular ≥ **~73%**.*
3. Baird DT, Horton R, Longcope C, Tait JF. Steroid dynamics under steady-state conditions. *Recent Prog Horm Res* 1969;25:611–664. doi:10.1016/b978-0-12-571125-8.50017-x. **HUMAN.** Review [4] cites it, together with [2], for the male E2 production rate and the testis share. *We did not read this one, so we quote no numbers from it directly.*
4. de Ronde W, de Jong FH. Aromatase inhibitors in men: effects and therapeutic options. *Reprod Biol Endocrinol* 2011;9:93. doi:10.1186/1477-7827-9-93. PMID 21693046 · PMC3143915 (full text checked). **HUMAN (review statement).** "The total estradiol production rate in the human male has been estimated to be **35–45 µg** (0.130–0.165 µmol) per day, of which **approximately 20%** is directly produced by the testes" (citing [3, 2]). "Roughly **60%** of circulating estradiol is derived from direct testicular secretion or from conversion of testicular androgens. The remaining fraction is derived from peripheral conversion of adrenal androgens" (citing [5]). Mean plasma E2 in men is "only about **1/200**" of mean plasma T. Aromatase activity has been shown in gonads and placenta and also in brain, fat, muscle, hair, bone and vascular tissue. *CYP19A1* is on chromosome 15 and "comprises nine exons," with the start codon in exon 2. It uses tissue-specific promoters: gonadal **PII**, driven by gonadotropins, and adipose/bone **I.4**, driven by IL-6, IL-11 and TNF-α. The enzyme sits in the ER. *Derived: ~80% not directly secreted, and ~40% from adrenal precursors.*
5. de Ronde W, Hofman A, Pols HA, de Jong FH. A direct approach to the estimation of the origin of oestrogens and androgens in elderly men by comparison with hormone levels in postmenopausal women. *Eur J Endocrinol* 2005;152:261–268. doi:10.1530/eje.1.01830. PMID 15745935. **HUMAN** (292 elderly men vs 367 postmenopausal women, stratified by DHEAS). Testis contribution (direct + indirect) = (C_men − C_women)/C_men. **E2: 72%, 60%, 52%, 44%** across DHEAS strata. **E1: 54–34%.** Androstenedione 0–14%. T 83–88%. Conclusion: "44–72% of E2 and 34–54% of E1 originate directly or indirectly from the testes." *This is an indirect, model-based estimate in elderly men. It is not a tracer study.*

## B. Direct testicular secretion (spermatic-vein gradients)
6. Kelch RP, Jenner MR, Weinstein R, Kaplan SL, Grumbach MM. Estradiol and testosterone secretion by human, simian, and canine testes… *J Clin Invest* 1972;51:824–830. doi:10.1172/jci106877. PMID 4259253 · PMC302196. **HUMAN** (8 normal men; monkey and dog for comparison). Peripheral-vein E2 **20 ± 1.6 pg/mL**. Spermatic-vein E2 **1049 ± 57 pg/mL**, about **50×** higher. The testicular T gradient is **110×** in humans. Individual adult male E2 values were 8–28 pg/mL (RIA, 1972 method). The authors call this "direct evidence that the human testis secretes estradiol." *Derived: 20 pg/mL ≈ 73 pmol/L.*
7. Weinstein RL, Kelch RP, Jenner MR, Kaplan SL, Grumbach MM. Secretion of unconjugated androgens and estrogens by the normal and abnormal human testis before and after hCG. *J Clin Invest* 1974;53:1–6. doi:10.1172/jci107526. PMID 4271572 · PMC301431. **HUMAN** (14 normal men). Spermatic venous **E2 1298 ± 216 pg/mL**, **E1 306 ± 55 pg/mL**, T 751 ± 114 ng/mL. "Testicular secretion of E1 accounts for **less than 5%** of E1 production in normal men." hCG raised testicular E2 secretion **8×** (hCG is used here as an experimental stimulus, not as treatment).
8. Longcope C, Widrich W, Sawin CT. The secretion of estrone and estradiol-17β by human testis. *Steroids* 1972;20:439–448. doi:10.1016/0039-128x(72)90042-6. PMID 5085131. **HUMAN.** *No abstract in PubMed or Europe PMC, so we quote no numbers. Read the full text before using it.*

## C. Where peripheral aromatization happens (tissue map)
9. Longcope C, Pratt JH, Schneider SH, Fineberg SE. Aromatization of androgens by muscle and adipose tissue in vivo. *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 1978;46:146–152. doi:10.1210/jcem-46-1-146. PMID 752017. **HUMAN** (15 normal men; forearm arteriovenous tracer sampling). Fractional conversion T→E2: **muscle 0.0007 ± 0.0001**, **adipose 0.0012 ± 0.0002**. Both are lower than the corresponding androstenedione→E1 values. If tissue aromatization is assumed constant throughout the body, **muscle accounts for 25–30%** and **adipose for 10–15%** of total extragonadal aromatization. *The androstenedione numbers are garbled in both indexed abstracts, so we don't quote them.*
10. Schweikert HU, Wolf L, Romalo G. Oestrogen formation from androstenedione in human bone. *Clin Endocrinol (Oxf)* 1995;43:37–42. doi:10.1111/j.1365-2265.1995.tb01890.x. PMID 7641410. **HUMAN** (spongiosa from 4 men and 11 postmenopausal women). Bone aromatase apparent **Km 6–50 nM (20.4 ± 3.9)**, "in the range of" placental microsomes. **Vmax 0.14–1.23 nmol/g DNA/h.** Background sentence: peripheral aromatization of T and androstenedione "is the principal source for circulating oestrogens in men."
11. Sasano H, Uzuki M, Sawai T, et al., Harada N. Aromatase in human bone tissue. *J Bone Miner Res* 1997;12:1416–1423. doi:10.1359/jbmr.1997.12.9.1416. PMID 9286757. **HUMAN** (IHC/ISH in 16 tibiae; RT-PCR in 28 rib, femur and vertebra samples from 16 men and 12 women). Aromatase and 17β-HSD1 were found in **lining cells, osteoblasts, articular chondrocytes and adipocytes next to trabeculae**. mRNA **11.25 ± 9.77 amol/ng RNA** (range 0.61–42.84), with no correlation to age or sex. The fibroblast-type exon **1b** was used in 23 of 26 samples.
12. Stoffel-Wagner B, Watzka M, Schramm J, Bidlingmaier F, Klingmüller D. Expression of CYP19 (aromatase) mRNA in different areas of the human brain. *J Steroid Biochem Mol Biol* 1999;70:237–241. doi:10.1016/s0960-0760(99)00114-4. PMID 10622413. **HUMAN** (surgical biopsies, 54 men and 45 women with epilepsy). CYP19 mRNA levels: **temporal neocortex 2.29 ± 0.40 AU** > frontal 0.92 ± 0.17 AU (P < 0.04), hippocampus 1.41 ± 0.18 AU. **No sex differences.** *These are arbitrary units, so compare only within the study. The tissue came from an epilepsy cohort.*
13. Brodie A, Inkster S, Yue W. Aromatase expression in the human male. *Mol Cell Endocrinol* 2001;178:23–28. doi:10.1016/s0303-7207(01)00444-0. PMID 11403890. **HUMAN** testis (aged 3 months to 72 years). In mature testis, aromatase immunostain was "always associated with **Leydig cells**" and absent from Sertoli cells. Activity **0.014–0.55 pmol estrogen/mg/h**, correlated with stain intensity, with no age trend. The highest levels were in men aged 18–20 years. Prepubertal testis: low activity, no stain.
14. Carreau S, Lambard S, Delalande C, et al. Aromatase expression and role of estrogens in male gonad: a review. *Reprod Biol Endocrinol* 2003;1:35. doi:10.1186/1477-7827-1-35. PMID 12747806 · PMC155680. **RAT** (Leydig cells, germ cells, elongated spermatids) and **HUMAN** (aromatase mRNA and protein in ejaculated spermatozoa). Promoter **PII** is "the main one" in testis. Germ-cell aromatase activity is 2–4× greater in spermatozoa than in younger germ cells (RAT).
15. Bulun SE, Simpson ER. Competitive RT-PCR analysis indicates that levels of aromatase P450 transcripts in adipose tissue of buttocks, thighs, and abdomen of women increase with advancing age. *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 1994;78:428–432. doi:10.1210/jcem.78.2.8106632. PMID 8106632. **HUMAN, WOMEN ONLY** (11 women aged 23–61). Adipose transcripts were **highest in buttocks > thighs > abdomen** and **2–4×** higher in older women. *We include this only as the adipose-depot map. No male equivalent was found this pass.*
16. Harada N, Utsumi T, Takagi Y. Tissue-specific expression of the human aromatase cytochrome P-450 gene by alternative use of multiple exons 1 and promoters… *PNAS* 1993;90:11312–11316. doi:10.1073/pnas.90.23.11312. PMID 8248245 · PMC47972. **HUMAN.** Exons 2–10 are identical in every tissue, and a **tissue-specific exon 1/promoter** is spliced on. This is "one enzyme, many switches." Most tissues splice 10 exons, the prostate 9, and the placenta 10 or 11. An adipose exon-1 switch was seen in 3 of 5 breast-cancer adipose samples.

## D. The enzyme itself (structure and stoichiometry)
17. Ghosh D, Griswold J, Erman M, Pangborn W. Structural basis for androgen specificity and oestrogen synthesis in human aromatase. *Nature* 2009;457:219–223. doi:10.1038/nature07614. PMID 19129847 · PMC2820300 (full text checked). **HUMAN** (term placental enzyme, 2.90 Å crystal structure with androstenedione). Aromatase is the only vertebrate enzyme known to make all estrogens from androgens. It works in **three steps, each using 1 O₂ + 1 NADPH**, coupled to cytochrome P450 reductase. Products: **androstenedione → estrone**, **testosterone → 17β-estradiol** and **16α-OH-T → estriol**. Steps 1 and 2 are **C19-methyl hydroxylations**, giving the C19-aldehyde via a 19,19-gem-diol. Step 3 **aromatizes ring A**. The gene is ***CYP19A1*, 15q21.1**, encoding **503 aa** plus a haem. The protein is ER-membrane anchored and has an androgen-specific cleft (Asp309 sits 2.6 Å from the 3-keto O).
18. Simpson ER, Mahendroo MS, Means GD, et al. Aromatase cytochrome P450, the enzyme responsible for estrogen biosynthesis. *Endocr Rev* 1994;15:342–355. doi:10.1210/edrv-15-3-342. PMID 8076586. *No abstract, so we cite it as a background review only.*
19. Simpson ER, Clyne C, Rubin G, et al. Aromatase—a brief overview. *Annu Rev Physiol* 2002;64:93–127. doi:10.1146/annurev.physiol.64.081601.142703. PMID 11826265. **Review** (already cited in paper 01). "In postmenopausal women, as in men," estrogen is made at extragonadal sites (breast, bone, vasculature, brain) and acts locally. Local aromatase "can generate high levels of estradiol locally **without significantly affecting circulating levels**." Expression is controlled by **tissue-specific promoters**.
20. Thompson EA, Siiteri PK. Utilization of oxygen and NADPH by human placental microsomes during aromatization of androstenedione. *J Biol Chem* 1974;249:5364–5372. doi:10.1016/s0021-9258(20)79735-8. PMID 4153532. **HUMAN** placental microsomes. *No abstract. It is the classic stoichiometry paper, cited here for history only. The 3 O₂ / 3 NADPH numbers are taken from [17].*

## UNSOURCED (explicitly not claimed)
- The textbook figure "**~85%** of male E2 from peripheral aromatization." We found no checked primary source that states it.
- That the C19 carbon is released as **formic acid**. This is a textbook claim that we did not verify this pass. [17] states only the three oxidative steps.
- **kcat / Vmax of purified human aromatase** for T vs androstenedione. Only bone-tissue Km/Vmax [10] are cited.
- **Male** adipose CYP19 by depot, and how obesity changes the male E2 production rate (no checked primary source this pass).
- **Leydig vs germ-cell share** of testicular E2 in humans.
- A **modern LC-MS/MS male E2 reference range** tied to production. This was left out on purpose and belongs to the soy WP's clinical sections. The only value used here is Kelch 1972's RIA mean [6], and it is labelled as such.
- Age trend of male extraglandular aromatization. Hemsell 1974 (PMID 4815174) has no abstract.
