# Research notes: Soy, estrogen and exogenous sex steroids
### Polish male frame, ages 0–25 · East Asia · Historical record · Female mirror

**Status:** research complete as of 2026-11-02 (Europe/Zagreb).

**Bibliography:** `sources/soy-estrogen-male.md`. Numbers in [brackets] below are entry numbers in that file.

**Rules applied:**
- Every number below carries a PMID, DOI or official report.
- Anything without one is marked **UNSOURCED**.
- Any number computed here is marked **DERIVED** and shows its arithmetic.
- **Evidence tags:** HUMAN-RCT / HUMAN-MA / HUMAN-OBS / HUMAN-CASE / ANIMAL / IN-VITRO / GUIDE / HIST.
- **Molecule classes stay separate throughout:**
  - **Phytoestrogens:** genistein, daidzein and equol (a gut-bacterial metabolite of daidzein). Plant or microbial isoflavones.
  - **Pharmaceutical estrogens:** estradiol (E2) in pills, patches, gels and creams; ethinylestradiol (EE2); diethylstilbestrol (DES).
  - **Cosmetic and incidental estrogenic exposures:** lavender and tea tree oils, hop 8-prenylnaringenin, adulterated supplements.
- No clinical advice is given anywhere in these notes.

## 1. Core chemistry (what makes a phytoestrogen different from estradiol)

| Item | Value | Source | Tag |

| Genistein | Binds ERβ about as well as estrone does; prefers ERβ over ERα | [1] | IN-VITRO |
| ERβ potency order | E2 ≫ genistein = coumestrol > zearalenone > daidzein | [2] | IN-VITRO |
| Concentration needed to switch on transcription | Phytoestrogens 1–10 nM; industrial chemicals (BPA etc.) 100–1000 nM | [2] | IN-VITRO |
| Equol | Made by gut bacteria from daidzein; binds ERα and ERβ; the S-(−) form prefers ERβ | Setchell 2002, PMID 12468591 [3]; Setchell & Clerici 2010, PMID 20519412 [4] | REVIEW |
| Equol producers | 25–30% of Western adults vs 50–60% in Japan, Korea and China | [4] | REVIEW |
| Plasma levels on 40 mg/d isoflavones (men) | Genistein ~1 µM, daidzein ~0.5 µM | Mitchell 2001, PMID 11352776 [10] | HUMAN |
| Hops 8-prenylnaringenin | A potent phytoestrogen; the amount in beer is low | Milligan 1999, PMID 10372741 [5] | IN-VITRO |

**Reading:** a weak, ERβ-leaning ligand present at high concentration is not the same thing as a strong ligand at picomolar to nanomolar concentration. Receptor affinity alone does not predict effects in the whole body.
