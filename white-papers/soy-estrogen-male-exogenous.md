## Abstract

The word "estrogen" covers two very different things. The first is a family of plant molecules: the soy **isoflavones** genistein and daidzein, plus **equol**, which some people's gut bacteria make from daidzein. The second is the body's own hormone **estradiol** and its pharmaceutical relatives: estradiol tablets, patches, gels and creams, ethinylestradiol, and diethylstilbestrol (DES). Both kinds fit into the same receptors. The resemblance ends there.

This paper follows one reference body, a Polish male, from birth to age 25, and asks what each kind does to his reproductive system, his breast tissue, and his capacity to lactate, and how timing matters during development.

**Main findings:**
- **Polish diet.** Measured isoflavone intake is about **0.16 mg/day** (Witkowska et al. 2015, PMID 26569297). That is roughly a hundredth of a Japanese man's median of **18 mg/day** (Food Safety Commission of Japan, 2006).
- **Adult men.** At the doses people actually eat, soy and isoflavones do **not** change testosterone, free testosterone, estradiol or SHBG. A 41-study meta-analysis found no effect at any dose or duration tested (Reed et al. 2021, PMID 33383165).
- **Semen.** The data are mixed: one small randomized trial and one intervention study were null, while two observational studies show a signal.
- **Soy-related gynecomastia** appears only in case reports of extreme intake.
- **Pharmacological estradiol** is in another league. Transdermal estradiol drove testosterone to castrate levels in **93%** of men at 3 months and caused gynecomastia in **86%** (Langley et al. 2021, PMID 33581820). Second-hand estradiol gel from a family member can trigger breast growth or early puberty in a child (Ng et al. 2026, PMID 42225386).
- **East Asia.** A section explains why lifelong soy eaters in Japan and China show no population-level feminization signal, and sets that against modern pharmacological estrogen use there.
- **History.** A section separates documented fact from legend. The "elixir of life" thread runs from Brown-Séquard's 1889 testicular extract (later shown to be placebo) to the 1935 synthesis of testosterone, which was done partly in Gdańsk. DES factory gynecomastia is documented; medieval Chinese "urinary steroids" are contested; yang-sheng feminization elixirs are unsourced.
- **Female mirror.** A parallel section covers what exogenous testosterone does organ by organ: the breast gland atrophies, terminal hair grows, voice pitch falls, lower-face soft tissue gains volume, the endometrium mostly thins, and the ovaries enlarge in most studies. Adult jaw-bone and Adam's-apple changes remain unmeasured.

## 1. Two kinds of "estrogen"

### 1.1 The receptor is a lock with two shapes

Estrogens act mostly through two nuclear receptors, **ERα** and **ERβ**. These are proteins that sit in the cell, bind a hormone, and then switch genes on or off. The two receptors are distributed differently across tissues and do not respond identically to every key.

The body's main estrogen, **17β-estradiol (E2)**, binds both receptors very tightly. In the classic binding study, E2's dissociation constant (Kd, the concentration at which half the receptors are occupied) was about **0.1 nM for ERα and 0.4 nM for ERβ** (Kuiper et al. 1997, PMID 9048584). A follow-up put E2's Kd at **0.05–0.1 nM** (Kuiper et al. 1998, PMID 9751507). One nanomolar is one billionth of a mole per litre. Estradiol works at a fraction of that.

### 1.2 The plant keys

Soybeans carry **isoflavones**, mostly as sugar-bound glycosides that the gut strips down to the active "aglycone" forms **genistein** and **daidzein**.
- **Genistein** prefers ERβ over ERα. It binds ERβ about as well as estrone, a weaker human estrogen (Kuiper et al. 1997).
- **Potency ranking.** At ERβ the order was E2 ≫ genistein = coumestrol > zearalenone > daidzein (Kuiper et al. 1998).
- **Transcription threshold.** Phytoestrogens began to activate gene transcription at **1–10 nM**. Industrial chemicals such as bisphenol A needed **100–1000 nM** (Kuiper et al. 1998). **IN-VITRO**
- **Equol.** Some people's gut bacteria convert daidzein to equol, which binds both receptors; its natural S-(−) form leans toward ERβ (Setchell et al. 2002, PMID 12468591; Setchell & Clerici 2010, PMID 20519412). About **25–30%** of Western adults are equol producers, compared with **50–60%** in Japan, Korea and China (Setchell & Clerici 2010). This matters for the East Asia section: identical soy can produce different chemistry in different guts.

### 1.3 Why concentration is not effect

A soy eater's blood can hold far more isoflavone than estradiol. In UK men taking **40 mg/day** of isoflavones for two months, plasma genistein reached about **1 µM** and daidzein about **0.5 µM**, roughly a thousand times nanomolar E2 levels. Endocrine measures, testicular volume and semen did not change (Mitchell et al. 2001, PMID 11352776). **HUMAN**

Three facts explain the gap:
1. Isoflavones bind each receptor less tightly than E2.
2. They prefer ERβ, whose actions often counter ERα's.
3. Many tissues that drive visible feminization, such as the breast gland and the pituitary's feedback on testosterone, depend mainly on ERα, which isoflavones touch only lightly.

Receptor tissue mapping beyond this is in the **Receptors** paper (Theme 3). Affinity and ERβ preference for genistein/daidzein/equol: library card [`library/receptors/esr2-equol.md`](../library/receptors/esr2-equol.md). The precise in-vivo potency ratio between dietary isoflavones and E2 has no single agreed number: **UNSOURCED** as one figure.

### 1.4 The pharmaceutical and cosmetic keys

**Pharmaceutical estrogens:**
- **Estradiol (E2):** oral tablets, transdermal patches, gels, sprays, vaginal creams. Chemically identical to the body's own.
- **Ethinylestradiol (EE2):** a synthetic estrogen resistant to liver breakdown, used in contraceptives and some cancer regimens.
- **Diethylstilbestrol (DES):** a non-steroidal synthetic first made in 1938 (Dodds et al. 1938, doi:10.1038/141247b0).

**Cosmetic and incidental estrogenic agents:**
- lavender and tea tree oils, which showed estrogenic and anti-androgenic activity in human cell lines (Henley et al. 2007, PMID 17267908)
- **8-prenylnaringenin** from hops, a potent phytoestrogen present in beer only at low levels (Milligan et al. 1999, PMID 10372741)
- illegally adulterated "herbal" products, such as a supplement spiked with DES (Toorians et al. 2010, doi:10.1080/19440041003660869)

**Takeaway:** the body hears three registers of estrogen: weak plant keys at high concentration, strong human or synthetic keys at low concentration, and odd-shaped cosmetic or contaminant keys. The rest of this paper keeps them in separate columns.

**Second-hand skin contact.** Topical estradiol does not stay where it is applied.
- A man developed gynecomastia from his partner's vaginal estrogen cream (DiRaimondo et al. 1980, PMID 7366629).
- In 2026, a five-year-old girl who co-slept with her mother reached Tanner breast stage 3, with bone age 4 years 6 months ahead. Her mother had used estradiol gel for 18 months. The changes regressed after the mother switched to a patch (Ng et al. 2026, PMID 42225386). **HUMAN-CASE**
- The same pattern appears with testosterone gel: children were virilized by a parent's topical androgen (Kunz et al. 2004, PMID 15231947). See Section 10.

**Environment.** Adding the contraceptive estrogen EE2 to an experimental Canadian lake at **5–6 ng/L** feminized male fathead minnows and collapsed the population (Kidd et al. 2007, PMID 17517636). The population recovered after the additions stopped (Blanchfield et al. 2015, PMID 25658019). This is **ANIMAL** evidence for aquatic exposure. It gives no human dose.

## 3. Poland and the EU: how much is on the plate

### 3.1 Poland

The only Polish isoflavone intake figure found comes from the **WOBASZ** survey: 6,661 adults aged 20–74, studied in 2003–2005.
- Intake was assessed with a single 24-hour dietary recall.
- With the USDA database, mean isoflavone intake was **0.16 ± 0.88 mg/day**.
- The top USDA-attributed sources were coffee (41.2%), soy flour (16.8%) and soybeans (16.2%).
- With the Phenol-Explorer database, the same diets gave **0.03 ± 0.01 mg/day** (Witkowska et al. 2015, PMID 26569297). **HUMAN-OBS**

A second, much smaller Polish data point agrees on the order of magnitude: 56 ulcerative-colitis patients in remission had isoflavone intakes in the **microgram-per-day** range (Skolmowska et al. 2019, PMID 31426486). **HUMAN-OBS**, a patient sample, not the population.

Coffee topping the list mostly shows how little soy there is. Two databases differing five-fold also shows how uncertain such small numbers are.

**Not found (UNSOURCED):**
- national Polish soy-food consumption statistics (GUS / IŻŻ)
- the share of Polish infants fed soy formula
- soy in Polish school catering
- any Polish study linking phytoestrogens to semen quality or hormones in men

### 3.2 Europe

**EU-wide data only; Poland is not included in the following.**

| Survey | Population | Intake | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| EPIC 24-h recall | 36,037 adults aged 35–74 in 10 countries | Total phytoestrogens from 24.9 mg/day (UK health-conscious men) down to 1.3 mg/day (Greek men). Higher in the north; lignans, not isoflavones, dominate outside the UK. | Zamora-Ros et al. 2012, PMID 22510793 |
| VENUS | Ireland, Italy, Netherlands, UK | Isoflavones **<1 mg/day** | van Erp-Baart et al. 2003, PMID 12725653 |

**EFSA 2015 scope and findings.** EFSA assessed isoflavone *supplements* for peri- and post-menopausal women, not men or children.
- No uterine effects up to 30 months at 150 mg/day.
- Some non-malignant changes after 60 months.
- No single health-based guidance value could be set.

There is **no EFSA assessment for men or boys**.

**Hormones in meat.** Using steroid hormones in meat production is banned in the EU (Partsch & Sippell 2001, PMID 11392376). Measured residue levels in Polish meat or dairy: **UNSOURCED**.

**Bisphenol A.** EFSA re-evaluated BPA in 2023 (doi:10.2903/j.efsa.2023.6857). Its tolerable daily intake value is not quoted here because it was not re-verified this session.

**Takeaway:** a Polish man's diet carries a fraction of a milligram of isoflavones a day. Any soy effect in Poland would have to come from deliberate high intake: supplements, heavy soy-milk use, or soy formula in infancy.

## 4. The adult male reproductive system

### 4.1 The control loop

Testosterone output is run by the **HPG axis** (hypothalamus–pituitary–gonad):
1. The hypothalamus pulses out GnRH.
2. The pituitary releases LH and FSH.
3. LH drives Leydig cells in the testis to make testosterone; FSH supports sperm production.
4. Estradiol, much of it made from testosterone by the enzyme aromatase, feeds back to throttle the loop.

Any strong estrogen entering from outside will therefore lower LH and, with it, testosterone. That is exactly how pharmacological estradiol works as a prostate-cancer therapy (Section 4.4). Full axis chemistry is in the **Biology & Chemistry** paper (Theme 1, §7).

### 4.1a Estrogen is already a male hormone

"Estrogen in a man" is not a foreign signal. The questions are **dose, potency, ratio to androgen, and timing**.
- **Splitting the two hormones.** Healthy men aged 20–50 had their own sex steroids shut down (goserelin) and then received graded testosterone, with or without the aromatase blocker anastrozole. Losses of lean mass, thigh-muscle size and strength tracked **low androgen**. The rise in body fat tracked **low estrogen** (Finkelstein et al. 2013, PMID 24024838). **HUMAN-RCT**
- **When a man cannot make or sense estrogen.**
  - In a man who could not make estrogen (aromatase deficiency), bone age was 14 years at age 24, with striking osteopenia (Morishima et al. 1995, PMID 8530621).
  - A 28-year-old man with a disrupted estrogen-receptor gene was **204 cm** tall with incomplete epiphyseal closure and was still growing (Smith et al. 1994, PMID 8090165).
  - **HUMAN-CASE**. Male bone maturation needs estrogen (review: Rochira et al. 2005, PMID 15685347).
- **Where a man's estradiol comes from (BioChem pointer).** Tracer studies put male E2 production at about **44 µg/day**. At most 12 µg of that is secreted directly by the testis, so **at least ~73% is made outside it** by aromatase (CYP19A1) in muscle, fat, bone and brain (derived from MacDonald et al. 1979, PMID 511978; review figure ~20% directly from testes, de Ronde & de Jong 2011, PMID 21693046). Most of that peripheral E2 is still built from testicular testosterone: about 60% of circulating E2 depends on the testis (de Ronde 2011; de Ronde et al. 2005, PMID 15745935). This "origin share" is a different number from the ~0.2–0.4% of testosterone output that becomes E2 (Longcope et al. 1969, PMID 5355335). **HUMAN** · Numbers and tags → [`../notes/male-aromatase-cyp19.md`](../notes/male-aromatase-cyp19.md) §3–4. The textbook "85%" figure remains **UNSOURCED**.

### 4.2 Hormones: the meta-analytic answer

- **Reed 2021.** The most complete analysis pooled **41 clinical studies** (Reed et al. 2021, PMID 33383165). **HUMAN-MA**
  - Sample sizes: total testosterone n = 1,753; free testosterone n = 752; estradiol n = 1,000; estrone n = 239; SHBG n = 967.
  - Soy and isoflavones had no effect on any of these.
  - This held below and above **75 mg/day** isoflavones, and for exposure up to 12 weeks and longer.
- **Earlier work.** An earlier meta-analysis reached the same conclusion (Hamilton-Reeves et al. 2010, PMID 19524224).
- **Messina 2022.** A technical review of 417 reports (229 observational, 157 clinical, 32 systematic reviews or meta-analyses) concluded that soy foods and isoflavones do not meet endocrine-disruptor criteria and found no adverse effect on male testosterone, estrogen or sperm (Messina et al. 2022, PMID 33775173). **REVIEW**; check the funding disclosures before print.
- **Messina 2010.** A critical review found that isoflavones do not affect testosterone and have essentially no effect on circulating estrogens across nine studies. It also noted that rodent erectile-dysfunction data do not translate to men (Messina 2010, PMID 20378106).

**Strength: STRONG.**

For scale, 75 mg/day is about the 95th percentile of Japanese male intake (76 mg/day; FSC 2006) and roughly **470 times** the Polish mean. **DERIVED:** 75 ÷ 0.16.

### 4.3 Semen

Semen evidence is thinner and **MIXED**.

| Study | Design | Exposure | Result | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beaton et al. 2010, PMID 19819436 | Crossover RCT, young men (~27.5 y) | Low-isoflavone soy protein 1.64 mg/day vs high-isoflavone soy protein 61.7 mg/day vs milk protein; 57 days each | No effect on any semen parameter | HUMAN-RCT |
| Mitchell et al. 2001, PMID 11352776 | Uncontrolled intervention | 40 mg/day for 2 months | No change in semen, testicular volume or hormones | HUMAN |
| Chavarro et al. 2008, PMID 18650557 | Cross-sectional, infertility clinic (n = 99) | Food-frequency soy | Highest soy group: −41 million sperm/mL (95% CI −74 to −8); no link to motility, morphology or volume | HUMAN-OBS |
| Xia et al. 2013, PMID 23820060 | Case-control, China (608 infertile vs 469 fertile) | Urinary isoflavones | Daidzein, genistein and the lignan secoisolariciresinol associated with idiopathic infertility (p-trend 0.036, 0.002, 0.0001) | HUMAN-OBS |

**Limits:**
- The trials are small and short. Messina (2010) counted only three semen intervention studies, none longer than three months.
- Sperm take more than two months to mature, so short trials may miss late effects (the exact cycle length is not cited here: **UNSOURCED**).
- The observational studies cannot exclude reverse causation or confounding, for example men attending infertility clinics changing their diets.

### 4.4 When the estrogen is pharmacological

In the UK **PATCH** randomized trials, men with prostate cancer were given either transdermal estradiol patches (four 100 µg patches during induction) or standard LHRH-agonist injections (Langley et al. 2021, PMID 33581820). **HUMAN-RCT**
- Estradiol reached castrate testosterone levels in **83%** of men at one month and **93%** at three months.
- Gynecomastia occurred in **86%** (690 of 807) on estradiol vs **38%** on LHRH agonists.
- Hot flushes occurred in **35%** vs **86%**.
- Long-term cardiovascular events did not differ (HR 1.11).

**Testicular tissue under long-term estradiol.** In 108 trans women examined at surgery, testicular histology varied widely, and about **24%** still showed qualitatively normal spermatogenesis (Schneider et al. 2015, PMID 26559385). **HUMAN-OBS**

### 4.5 Case reports at the extreme

Two published cases link very high soy intake to male endocrine change. **HUMAN-CASE**; neither proves causation.
- **Martinez & Lewi (2008), PMID 18558591.** A 60-year-old man drank about **3 quarts (~2.8 L) of soy milk a day**. His estrone and estradiol were about **four times** the upper reference limit, and he had gynecomastia. Both resolved after he stopped.
- **Siepmann et al. (2011), PMID 21353476.** A 19-year-old man with a high soy intake had low free and total testosterone, raised DHEA and erectile dysfunction. These normalized within a year of stopping.

For scale, using Messina's (2024) conversion of 240 mL soy milk ≈ 25 mg isoflavones (PMID 38484974), 2.8 L/day would carry about **290 mg/day**. **DERIVED:** 2,800 ÷ 240 × 25 ≈ 292. That is far above any population intake in Section 3 or Section 8.

## 5. Gynecomastia

### 5.1 What it is

**Gynecomastia** is growth of true glandular breast tissue in a male, as distinct from fat. The breast gland grows when estrogen action outweighs androgen action. Estrogens stimulate the tissue and androgens restrain it, so the **estrogen-to-androgen balance** decides the outcome rather than either hormone alone (Narula & Carlson 2014, PMID 25112235; Braunstein 2007, PMID 17881754).

### 5.2 The normal baseline

In **6,200 Bulgarian boys** (Kumanov et al. 2007, PMID 17659215; **HUMAN-OBS**):
- Gynecomastia of at least 1 cm appeared only after age 10.
- Prevalence was **3.93%** between ages 10 and 19.
- It clustered at Tanner stages 3–4 and was associated with varicocele (OR 2.1).

This is the closest Central/Eastern European baseline found. A Polish prevalence figure is **UNSOURCED**.

## 6. Lactation and galactorrhea

**Lactation** is milk production. **Galactorrhea** is milk discharge outside nursing. Milk synthesis is driven mainly by **prolactin** from the pituitary, primed by estrogen and progesterone. Hyperprolactinemia is the common clinical cause of galactorrhea (Melmed et al. 2011, PMID 21296991).

- **Newborns.** "Witch's milk" was seen in **4.6%** of examinations (38 of 640 infants) and was still present in 1.8% at two months, with no sex difference (Madlon-Kay 1986, PMID 3946357). **HUMAN-OBS** It reflects maternal hormones crossing the placenta. Newborn boys can briefly lactate.
- **Induced lactation in an adult with male-typical anatomy.** A 30-year-old trans woman who had been on estradiol for six years used domperidone, estradiol, progesterone and breast pumping. She produced enough milk to be her infant's sole food for **six weeks** (Reisman & Goldstein 2018, PMID 29372185). **HUMAN-CASE** Male breast tissue can make milk, but only when estrogen, progestin, a prolactin stimulus and mechanical stimulation are deliberately combined.
- **Soy causing male galactorrhea:** no case or study found. **ABSENT.**
- **Exogenous estradiol alone causing male galactorrhea:** no quantitative source found. **UNSOURCED.**

## 7. Ages 0–25: developmental windows

Development is when hormones *build* tissue rather than just adjusting it. The concern with soy formula is that it delivers the highest isoflavone dose per kilogram of any human diet at the age the reproductive axis is briefly active ("mini-puberty").

**Expert bodies on soy formula:**
- **ESPGHAN** (European pediatric gastroenterologists) said soy formula has no nutritional advantage over cow-milk formula and contains phytoestrogens, phytate and aluminium. They listed narrow indications: galactosemia, severe lactose intolerance, and vegan families (ESPGHAN 2006, PMID 16641572).
- **AAP.** The American Academy of Pediatrics noted soy formula held about 25% of the US market despite limited indications (Bhatia & Greer 2008, PMID 18450914).
- **Vandenplas et al. (2014).** A systematic review found no strong evidence of reproductive or endocrine harm (PMID 24507712).

This paper reports these positions and does not make feeding recommendations.

**Reading the window:**
- The largest human exposure (soy formula) has produced no consistent male reproductive signal in humans, despite a clear signal in marmosets.
- Where human data do show a signal, it is in girls (Adgent, Strom, Cheng).
- In puberty, more soy tends to mean slightly *later*, not earlier, timing in two of three cohorts.
- No Polish data exist for any of these windows: **UNSOURCED**.

### 8.3 Male puberty, reproduction and gynecomastia in East Asia

- **Puberty (China).** In 4,781 Chinese children, more soy meant slightly later puberty milestones in boys (genital stage 2 HR 0.91; voice break HR 0.90), with stronger effects in high equol producers (Xiong et al. 2022). **HUMAN-OBS**
- **Hormones (Japan).**
  - Among 69 Japanese men, soy intake correlated inversely with estradiol (r = −0.32, p = 0.009), with borderline inverse correlations for total and free testosterone (Nagata et al. 2000, PMID 10798211). **HUMAN-OBS**
  - In Japanese preschool boys, soy was inversely associated with urinary estrogens (Wada et al. 2011).
- **Fertility (China).** Urinary isoflavones were higher in 608 men with idiopathic infertility than in 469 fertile controls (Xia et al. 2013). **HUMAN-OBS**, **MIXED**.
- **Gynecomastia attributed to dietary soy in East Asian men:** no study or case found. **ABSENT.**

Read together: a population eating roughly a hundred times more isoflavone than Poland, with twice the share of equol producers, shows no documented feminization signal, small shifts in puberty timing toward *later*, and one contested fertility association. This is consistent with the meta-analyses in Section 4.

### 8.4 Modern pharmacological estrogens in East Asian males

- **Prostate cancer (Japan).** In 24 Japanese men with castration-resistant prostate cancer, oral **ethinylestradiol 1.5 mg/day** cut PSA by more than 50% in 70% of patients (Izumi et al. 2010, PMID 21187513). **HUMAN-OBS** EE2 is a potent synthetic estrogen; this is a cancer regimen, not a dietary exposure.
- **Gender-affirming hormones (China).** In a 2026 survey of 4,296 transgender people (1,462 trans men, 2,834 trans women; median age 21):
  - 87.5% wanted hormone therapy and 52.3% used it.
  - Only **15.1%** of hormone users had valid prescriptions.
  - Trans women were more likely to start before age 16.
  (Hou et al. 2026, PMID 41493781) **HUMAN-OBS** For the 0–25 frame, unsupervised pharmacological estradiol is a documented route of exposure in young East Asian males that dwarfs anything from diet.
- **Supplements (Japan, women).** About **5%** of Japanese women aged 15–69 use estrogen-like supplements (~15% including former users).
  - Soy/isoflavone products were most common, then equol, then placenta.
  - *Pueraria mirifica*, a Thai root whose phytoestrogen miroestrol is derived from daidzein (Suntichaikamolkul et al. 2023, PMID 36218384), was used mainly by teenagers and women in their twenties.
  - 16.2% reported adverse events.
  (Chiba et al. 2022, PMID 36364772) Equivalent male data: **UNSOURCED**.

### 8.5 The contrast: dietary isoflavone vs pharmacological estradiol

| | Japanese man, dietary soy | Man on transdermal estradiol (PATCH) | Trans woman on estradiol |
|---|---|---|---|
| Molecule | Genistein, daidzein (± equol) | 17β-estradiol | 17β-estradiol (± anti-androgen) |

## 10. Mirror: exogenous androgens and the female body

The paper so far asked what outside estrogens do to a male body. This section turns the question around: what do outside **androgens** do to a female body? It is secondary to the male frame, and its anatomy runs in parallel. The umbrella term for both directions is **exogenous sex steroids**.

### 10.1 Framing and regimens

**Routes of exposure:**
- gender-affirming testosterone in trans men
- anabolic-androgenic steroid (AAS) use by women athletes
- historical state doping
- second-hand contact with others' gels
- "testosterone foods"

**Guidelines.** Testosterone regimens for trans men are defined by the Endocrine Society guideline (Hembree et al. 2017, PMID 28945902). This paper describes biology only, so it does not reproduce doses or schedules. **SOC-8** (Coleman et al. 2022, PMID 36238954) is the international standard of care. Wierman et al. (2014, PMID 25279570) cover androgen therapy in cisgender women.

**Timing.** Most changes begin within months. Facial hair and scalp hair loss keep progressing beyond one year. Randomized trials are few (Irwig 2017, PMID 27084565). The guideline's expected timeline for **adults** (Hembree et al. 2017, Table 12; guideline estimates, not trial measurements):

| Effect | Onset | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Skin oiliness / acne | 1–6 months | 1–2 years |
| Facial and body hair | 6–12 months | 4–5 years |
| Scalp hair loss | 6–12 months | variable |
| Muscle mass and strength | 6–12 months | 2–5 years |
| Fat redistribution | 1–6 months | 2–5 years |
| Menses stop | 1–6 months | — |
| Vaginal atrophy | 1–6 months | 1–2 years |
| Voice deepening | 6–12 months | 1–2 years |
| Clitoral enlargement (minor line) | 1–6 months | 1–2 years |

**Developmental window.** Puberty suppression before testosterone prevents the original sex characteristics from developing (Hembree 2017). The 0–25 frame therefore applies here too.

### 10.2 Breast: suppression and atrophy

Testosterone does not reverse adult breast development on its own, but it does change the tissue. In 100 mastectomy specimens from trans men on long-term testosterone, **93%** showed marked glandular reduction with fibrosis (Grynberg et al. 2010, PMID 20122869). **HUMAN-OBS** This mirrors the male picture: androgens restrain the breast gland (Narula & Carlson 2014).
- Breast **volume** change in mL or cm on testosterone: **UNSOURCED**. No prospective measurement was found to match de Blok's male-to-female data.

### 10.3 Hair: body, face and scalp

Testosterone turns fine vellus hair into thick terminal hair in androgen-sensitive skin.
- In 17 trans men followed for a year, hair growth rate and skin sebum rose. At 12 months, cheek and abdominal hair diameters had **not yet reached male values** (Giltay & Gooren 2000, PMID 10946903). **HUMAN-OBS**
- Facial hair keeps developing after the first year, and androgenic scalp hair loss can follow (Irwig 2017).
- AAS-using women report facial hair growth (Strauss et al. 1985, PMID 3989963).
- Scalp loss rates: **UNSOURCED**.

### 10.4 Face and mandible

**Soft tissue.** Using 3D stereophotogrammetry in 18 people starting testosterone (17 trans men and 1 nonbinary person), total facial and mandibular **volume** increased. Most of the change was in the lower third of the face and the fastest change came in the first three months (Mélo et al. 2025, PMID 41068338). **HUMAN-OBS** This is surface soft tissue: fat, muscle and skin.

**Bone.** Whether adult mandibular or other craniofacial **bone** remodels under testosterone after growth plates close was not found measured: **UNSOURCED**. The idea that male jaw shape is set mainly at puberty is reasonable but not cited here: **UNSOURCED**. Skeletal sex differences are covered in **Bone Metrics** (Theme 4).

### 10.5 Larynx, voice and the Adam's apple

**Voice.** Testosterone thickens the vocal folds and pitch drops. In 50 trans men, mean speaking fundamental frequency fell to **125 Hz** at 12 months. **24%** reported voice symptoms, and pitch did not correlate with androgen levels (Nygren et al. 2016, PMID 26678122). **HUMAN-OBS** Some trans men later choose thyroplasty to lower pitch further (Bultynck et al. 2021, PMID 32600117), which shows the hormonal drop is not always complete.

**Adam's apple.** Whether the thyroid-cartilage prominence grows in adults is unmeasured: **UNSOURCED**. The timing of male laryngeal-angle formation is not cited here, and adult growth was not found quantified.

- Androgen-target tissues outside the pelvis (hair follicles, vocal folds, skin, muscle) grow.
- Estrogen-dependent tissues (breast gland, endometrium) mostly shrink or go quiet.
- The ovary is the paradox: under high testosterone it tends to **enlarge**, with a thickened cortex and stroma, even as it stops cycling.
- Uterine size itself does not reliably shrink.

### 10.7 Other routes

- **AAS in women.** Ten weight-trained women using AAS reported voice deepening, facial hair, clitoral enlargement and menstrual irregularity (Strauss et al. 1985). A 2018 review lists acne, hirsutism, voice deepening, menstrual disturbance and arrhythmias (Huang & Basaria 2018, PMID 28711608). Recruitment and help-seeking among female AAS users is described qualitatively (Börjesson et al. 2016, PMID 26945991). An Endocrine Society statement lists menstrual disturbance and virilization in women, and suppressed LH/FSH, testicular atrophy, infertility and gynecomastia in men (Pope et al. 2014, PMID 24423981).
- **Why it is done.** Circulating testosterone is considered the strongest single factor behind the **10–20%** male advantage in sport performance (Hirschberg 2020, PMID 32197237). **REVIEW**
- **State doping.** East Germany's program gave androgens to girls and women, including minors (Franke & Berendonk 1997).
- **Second-hand gel.** Young children were virilized after contact with a parent's topical testosterone (Kunz et al. 2004, PMID 15231947). This is the androgen mirror of the estradiol-gel case in Section 2.
- **"Testosterone foods" and folk androgens.**
  - Elk velvet antler at 560 mg/day for 10 weeks produced no hormonal difference from placebo in 25 men and 21 women (Syrotuik et al. 2005, PMID 16286669). **HUMAN-RCT**
  - Deer antler velvet produced no endocrine change in 38 men (Sleivert et al. 2003, PMID 14669926). **HUMAN-RCT**
  - Any food masculinizing women: **ABSENT**.
  - For contrast, the effect also runs the other way in diet. Spearmint tea *lowered* free and total testosterone in women with PCOS without significantly changing hirsutism scores (p = 0.12) (Grant 2010, PMID 19585478).
- **East Asian parallels.** Japanese ovarian histology under testosterone is documented (Ikeda 2013), as is Chinese trans men's hormone use, mostly unprescribed (Hou 2026). Historical East Asian masculinizing elixirs for women: **UNSOURCED / LEGEND**. Deer antler velvet (lù róng) is sold as a traditional tonic (historical use not sourced here), and the trials above found no hormonal effect.

## 12. How to read this on the map

This paper is a content source for the **Corpus Nova body map**. It does not ship its own viewer. Its payloads load onto the existing Human-Atlas layers: the BodyParts3D male mesh set and the HRA female organ set. Details are in `notes/soy-estrogen-male-interactive-plan.md`.

- **Pick a body.** The male body is the default. A sex toggle (already in `body-map/overlays/sex-layers.json`) switches to the female mirror.
- **Hover a hotspot.**
  - Male: **breast gland**, **testis**, **HPG** (hypothalamus + pituitary), **liver**.
  - Female: **breasts**, **hair** (face/body), **mandible/lower face**, **larynx**, **uterus**, **ovaries**. A clitoris pin is optional and minor.
  Each hover shows a 2–3-line card like the *Map panel* boxes above, ending in *Dig deeper →* with a section link.
- **Read the colour.** Hotspot fill follows the STYLE.md palette: signal teal (#2F6F6A) for STRONG, signal-soft (#DCEEEB) for MIXED/WEAK, a hatched outline for ABSENT/UNSOURCED.
- **Read the badge.** Each card carries **HUMAN** or **ANIMAL**, plus the molecule class (phytoestrogen / pharmaceutical / cosmetic / androgen).
- **Two sliders, not one.** The dose slider separates *dietary isoflavone* (mg/day) from *pharmacological estradiol* (µg/day) so a reader never compares them on one scale.
- **Motion.** Where motion helps, such as the HPG feedback loop or breast change over 12 months, it ships as a transparent **GIF**. Everything else is transparent **PNG**.
- **What's locked.** Atlas meshes and the viewer are fixed (Atlas Lock). This paper adds overlays only, and does not add 3D models, animation engines or VR.

## 13. Open edges

1. **Poland.** Soy consumption, soy-formula share, school meals, semen–phytoestrogen data, hormone residues in food: all **UNSOURCED**. Only WOBASZ intake exists.
2. **East Asian children.** mg/day soy intake by age: **UNSOURCED**.
3. **Long semen trials.** No soy trial runs longer than about three months.
4. **Female mirror sizes.** Breast volume (mL), clitoral size (mm), adult jaw bone, thyroid cartilage: **UNSOURCED**. Uterine and ovarian volume results conflict.
5. **History.** Yang-sheng feminization, Japanese elixirs, soy-for-libido traditions, Qin Shi Huang's cause of death: **UNSOURCED**. The qiu shi replication literature needs full citations. Male peripheral-aromatization share of E2: now sourced via BioChem [`notes/male-aromatase-cyp19.md`](../notes/male-aromatase-cyp19.md) (§4.1a); textbook "85%" stays **UNSOURCED**.
6. **Not yet verified, so not cited:** EFSA BPA TDI value; NTP-CERHR 2010 soy-formula monograph; FDA Evamist notice; licorice and testosterone; Senofonte 2019 venue.
